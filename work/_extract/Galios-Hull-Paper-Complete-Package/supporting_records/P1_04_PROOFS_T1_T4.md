## 0. Standing hypotheses and the dual-order convention (verbatim)

Throughout, one component is fixed and the subscript `s` is suppressed.

- **(S1)** `p` is prime, `K = F_{p^d}`, and `k` is an integer with `0 ≤ k ≤ d−1`.  
  `σ(α) = α^{p^k}` and `ρ(α) = α^{p^{d−k}}` for `α ∈ K`. Since `α^{p^d} = α` on `K`, `ρ = σ⁻¹` on `K`.
- **(S2)** `ϱ ≥ 0`, `P = p^ϱ ≥ 1`, `n = P·n_0` with `gcd(n_0, p) = 1`.
- **(S3)** `λ ∈ K*` satisfies the compatibility condition `λ^{1 + p^{d−k}} = 1`, and `μ ∈ K*` is the unique element with `μ^P = λ` (the map `a ↦ a^P` is a bijection of the finite field `K`).
- **(S4)** `M(x) = x^n − λ`, `S(x) = x^{n_0} − μ`. Then `M = S^P` (characteristic `p`: `(x^{n_0} − μ)^P = x^{n_0 P} − μ^P`), and `S` is separable with distinct monic irreducible factors: `S = ∏_{f∈F} f`, `M = ∏_{f∈F} f^P`. (Separability: `S′ = n_0 x^{n_0 − 1}`, `p ∤ n_0`, and every root `α` of `S` has `α^{n_0} = μ ≠ 0`, hence `α ≠ 0` and `S′(α) ≠ 0`.)
- **(S5)** Codes are `K`-linear subspaces of `K^n`, identified with polynomials of degree `< n`; a `λ`-constacyclic code is an ideal of `R = K[x]/(M)`. For `a = (a_f)_{f∈F} ∈ {0,…,P}^F`, `g_a = ∏_{f∈F} f^{a_f}` and `C(a) = ⟨g_a⟩ ⊆ R`; `h_a = M / g_a = ∏_{f∈F} f^{P − a_f}`.
- **(S6)** For monic `f(x) = Σ_{i=0}^r c_i x^i ∈ K[x]` with `c_0 ≠ 0` (automatic for `f ∈ F`, since `f | S` and `S(0) = −μ ≠ 0`), the reciprocal is  
  `f#(x) = ρ(c_0)^{-1} Σ_{i=0}^r ρ(c_i) x^{r−i}`, and `τ(f) = f#`.
- **(S7) Dual-order convention (stated verbatim, as required):**  
  “The code-first pairing is `⟨x, y⟩_k = Σ_{i=0}^{n−1} x_i σ(y_i)`, and the code-first dual is  
  `D(C) = { y ∈ K^n : ⟨c, y⟩_k = 0 for all c ∈ C }` — the code vector occupies the first slot and the candidate vector the second slot. The Euclidean dual is `C^⊥ = { z ∈ K^n : Σ_i c_i z_i = 0 for all c ∈ C }`, and `Hull_k(C) = C ∩ D(C)`.”  
  With this order, §5 shows `D(C) = ρ(C^⊥)` (coordinatewise `ρ`). Under the opposite order the roles of `σ` and `ρ` would interchange; that reading is **not** used anywhere in this file.
- **(S8)** A `τ`-orbit is enumerated `O = (f_0, …, f_{L−1})` with `τ(f_i) = f_{i+1}` (indices mod `L`), and `α_i := a_{f_i}`. The predecessor of `i` is `i−1`.

Dimensions are over `K` unless stated otherwise. If the component is read as the blueprint’s `K_s = F_{q^{m_s}}`, then `dim_{F_q} = m_s · dim_K` for every `K`-subspace below, because `[K : F_q] = m_s`.

Two foundational facts about `K[x]` are used and proved here, so that nothing is hidden behind the word “standard”:

**Fact 0.1 (division; `K[x]` is a principal ideal domain).** For `φ, ψ ∈ K[x]`, `ψ ≠ 0`, there exist unique `q, r` with `φ = qψ + r` and `r = 0` or `deg r < deg ψ` (polynomial long division; the leading coefficient of `ψ` is invertible because `K` is a field). Consequently every ideal `I ≠ (0)` of `K[x]` is principal, generated by any monic element of `I` of minimal degree: if `φ ∈ I`, divide `φ = qḡ + r`; then `r = φ − qḡ ∈ I` and minimality forces `r = 0`.

**Fact 0.2 (unique factorization).** Every nonconstant `φ ∈ K[x]` factors into monic irreducibles (induction on `deg φ`: either `φ` is irreducible or `φ = φ_1 φ_2` with both degrees smaller), and the factorization is unique up to order. Uniqueness uses Euclid’s lemma: if `π` is irreducible and `π | uv`, then `π | u` or `π | v`. Indeed, by Fact 0.1 the ideal `(π, u)` is generated by a common divisor of `π` and `u`; since `π` is irreducible this generator is `1` or an associate of `π`. If `π ∤ u`, the generator is `1`, so `Aπ + Bu = 1` for some `A, B`; multiplying by `v` gives `v = Aπv + B(uv)`, and `π` divides both terms on the right, hence `π | v`. Uniqueness then follows by cancelling one irreducible factor at a time (induction on the number of factors). For `π` irreducible, the valuation `v_π(φ)` is the exponent of `π` in this factorization; `φ | ψ` iff `v_π(φ) ≤ v_π(ψ)` for all `π`.

## 1. RR-1 — Divisor/exponent correspondence and the dimension formula

**Lemma RR-1.** Let `M ∈ K[x]` be monic of degree `n ≥ 1` with `M = ∏_{f∈F} f^{P}` the factorization of Fact 0.2 into distinct monic irreducibles (`P ≥ 0` an integer; in our setting `P = p^ϱ ≥ 1`).

(i) The map `a ↦ g_a = ∏_{f∈F} f^{a_f}` is a bijection from `{0,…,P}^F` onto the set of monic divisors of `M`.

(ii) The map `a ↦ ⟨g_a⟩ ⊆ K[x]/(M)` is a bijection from `{0,…,P}^F` onto the set of ideals of `K[x]/(M)`.

(iii) For every monic divisor `g` of `M`, `dim_K ⟨g⟩ = n − deg g` (the ideal read as the set of multiples of `g` of degree `< n`).

**Proof.** (i) If `a ∈ {0,…,P}^F`, then `g_a | M` with `v_f(g_a) = a_f ≤ P = v_f(M)` for `f ∈ F` and `v_π(g_a) = 0 = v_π(M)` for `π ∉ F`, by Fact 0.2. Conversely, if `g | M` is monic, then `v_f(g) ≤ v_f(M) = P` for `f ∈ F` and `v_π(g) = 0` for `π ∉ F`, so `g = ∏ f^{v_f(g)} = g_a` for `a_f := v_f(g)`. The two constructions are mutually inverse because Fact 0.2 makes the exponents `v_f(g)` intrinsic to `g`.

(ii) Ideals of `K[x]/(M)` correspond bijectively to ideals `I` of `K[x]` with `(M) ⊆ I` (preimage under the quotient map; this is the definition of the quotient ring’s ideals). By Fact 0.1, `I = (ḡ)` for a unique monic `ḡ` (the monic generator is unique: two generators differ by a unit of `K[x]`, i.e. a nonzero constant, and only one is monic). The inclusion `(M) ⊆ (ḡ)` holds iff `ḡ | M`. Hence ideals of the quotient correspond to monic divisors of `M`, and (i) finishes the proof. No hypothesis on `M` (in particular no separability or square-freeness) is used.

(iii) Let `g | M` be monic, `r = deg g`, and put `h = M/g` (a polynomial, of degree `n − r`). Consider the `K`-linear map `Φ : K[x]/(h) → (g)/(M)`, `[u] ↦ [gu]`. It is well defined (if `u − u′ ∈ (h)` then `g(u − u′) ∈ (gh) = (M)`), and it is surjective by the definition of `(g)/(M)`. It is injective: if `gu ∈ (M) = (gh)`, then `gh | gu` in `K[x]`, and cancelling `g` (Fact 0.2, or directly: `gu = gh·w` implies `u = hw` since `K[x]` is a domain) gives `u ∈ (h)`. Hence `Φ` is an isomorphism. Now `dim_K K[x]/(h) = deg h = n − r`: the classes of `1, x, …, x^{deg h − 1}` form a basis, because every polynomial is congruent modulo `h` to its remainder of degree `< deg h` (Fact 0.1, existence), and a polynomial of degree `< deg h` divisible by `h` is zero (Fact 0.1, degree bound). Therefore `dim_K ⟨g⟩ = n − deg g`. ∎

**Theorem T1 (parametrization, single component).** Under (S1)–(S5), the map `a ↦ C(a) = ⟨g_a⟩` is a bijection from `{0,…,P}^F` onto the set of `λ`-constacyclic codes of length `n` over `K`, and `dim_K C(a) = n − Σ_{f∈F} a_f·deg f`. In particular there are `(P+1)^{|F|}` such codes.

**Proof.** By (S5), `λ`-constacyclic codes are exactly the ideals of `R = K[x]/(M)`. Lemma RR-1(ii) with `M = ∏ f^P` (S4) gives the bijection, and RR-1(iii) gives `dim_K C(a) = n − deg g_a = n − Σ_f a_f deg f`. ∎

*Hypotheses actually used:* Fact 0.1–0.2 and `M = ∏ f^P`. Neither `gcd(n_0, p) = 1` nor compatibility (S3) is used in T1; they enter through (S4)’s guarantee that the distinct factors of `M` are exactly the factors of the separable polynomial `S`, each with multiplicity exactly `P`.

## 2. RR-2 — Coefficientwise `ρ` and the reciprocal `#`

Write `ρ̄ : K[x] → K[x]`, `ρ̄(Σ_i c_i x^i) = Σ_i ρ(c_i) x^i`, and let `σ̄` be the analogous map for `σ`.

**Lemma RR-2.** (i) `ρ̄` is a ring automorphism of `K[x]` with inverse `σ̄`.  
(ii) For monic `f` of degree `r` with `f(0) ≠ 0`, `f#` (S6) is monic of degree `r` with `f#(0) ≠ 0`; explicitly, if `f^♮_ρ := ρ(c_0)^{-1}·x^r ρ̄(f)(1/x)`, then `f^♮_ρ = f#`.  
(iii) `#` is multiplicative on monic polynomials with nonzero constant term: `(fg)# = f#·g#`. Hence `(∏_{f∈F} f^{a_f})# = ∏_{f∈F} (f#)^{a_f}` for any exponents `a_f ≥ 0`.  
(iv) (Root transport.) Let `f` be monic irreducible of degree `r` with `f(0) ≠ 0`, and let `α ∈ \bar K` (an algebraic closure of `K`) with `f(α) = 0`. Then `f#` is the minimal polynomial over `K` of `β := α^{−p^{d−k}}`; in particular `f#` is monic irreducible of degree `r` and `f#(β) = 0`.  
(v) (Compatibility, two forms.) `λ^{1 + p^{d−k}} = 1 ⟺ μ^{1 + p^{d−k}} = 1`.  
(vi) Under (S3)–(S4), `τ : f ↦ f#` maps `F` into `F` and is a permutation of `F`; all factors in one `τ`-orbit have the same degree.

**Proof.** (i) `ρ̄` preserves addition coefficientwise and preserves multiplication because `ρ` is a field homomorphism: the coefficient of `x^m` in `ρ̄(φψ)` is `ρ(Σ_{i+j=m} φ_i ψ_j) = Σ_{i+j=m} ρ(φ_i)ρ(ψ_j)`, which is the coefficient of `x^m` in `ρ̄(φ)ρ̄(ψ)`. Since `ρσ = σρ = id_K` (S1), `σ̄ρ̄ = ρ̄σ̄ = id`, so `ρ̄` is an automorphism with inverse `σ̄`.

(ii) With `f = Σ_{i=0}^r c_i x^i`, `c_r = 1`: `x^r ρ̄(f)(1/x) = Σ_{i=0}^r ρ(c_i) x^{r−i}`. Its coefficient of `x^r` is `ρ(c_0)`; multiplying by `ρ(c_0)^{-1}` gives exactly (S6). The result is monic (leading coefficient `ρ(c_0)^{-1}ρ(c_0) = 1`), of degree `r` (leading coefficient nonzero), and its constant term is `ρ(c_0)^{-1}ρ(c_r) = ρ(c_0)^{-1} ≠ 0`.

(iii) Let `f, g` be monic of degrees `r, t` with nonzero constant terms. Applying the ring automorphism `ρ̄` to the product gives `ρ̄(fg) = ρ̄(f)ρ̄(g)`. Reversal is multiplicative on polynomials of fixed degrees: `x^{r+t}(ρ̄(fg))(1/x) = [x^r ρ̄(f)(1/x)]·[x^t ρ̄(g)(1/x)]`, because both sides equal `x^{r+t}ρ̄(f)(1/x)ρ̄(g)(1/x)`. The normalizing constants also multiply: `(fg)_0 = f_0 g_0`, so `ρ((fg)_0)^{-1} = ρ(f_0)^{-1}ρ(g_0)^{-1}`. Combining the three identities and using (ii) gives `(fg)# = f# g#`. The displayed consequence follows by induction on the number of factors (with `a_f = 0` contributing the empty factor `1`, and `(f^a)# = (f#)^a` by induction on `a`).

(iv) Let `Φ : \bar K → \bar K`, `Φ(t) = t^{p^{d−k}}`. Then `Φ` is a field endomorphism of `\bar K` (Frobenius in characteristic `p`), injective (a field homomorphism out of a field is injective or zero, and `Φ(1) = 1`), and surjective because `\bar K` is algebraically closed, so `X^{p^{d−k}} − t` has a root in `\bar K` for every `t`; hence `Φ` is an automorphism of `\bar K`. On `K = F_{p^d}`, `Φ(c) = c^{p^{d−k}} = ρ(c)`. Note `α ≠ 0` because `f(0) ≠ 0`, so `β = Φ(α)^{-1}` is defined. First, `f#(β) = 0`: applying `Φ` to `f(α) = Σ c_i α^i = 0` gives `Σ ρ(c_i) Φ(α)^i = 0`, i.e. `Σ ρ(c_i) β^{−i} = 0`; multiplying by `β^r` gives `Σ ρ(c_i) β^{r−i} = 0`, which is `ρ(c_0)·f#(β) = 0` by (ii), and `ρ(c_0) ≠ 0`. Next, degrees: `Φ` maps `K(α)` onto `K(Φ(α))` (it maps the coefficient field `K` onto itself and the generator `α` to `Φ(α)`), and it preserves the degree: if `Σ_{i=0}^{r−1} e_i Φ(α)^i = 0` with `e_i ∈ K`, write `e_i = Φ(d_i)` with `d_i = σ(e_i) ∈ K` and apply `Φ⁻¹` to get `Σ d_i α^i = 0`, so every `d_i = 0` because `[K(α) : K] = r` (`f` is the minimal polynomial of `α`, being monic irreducible with `f(α) = 0`); spanning is identical. Hence `[K(Φ(α)) : K] = r`, and `K(β) = K(Φ(α))` (as `β = Φ(α)^{-1}`), so the minimal polynomial `m_β` of `β` over `K` has degree `r`. Since `m_β | f#` (because `f#(β) = 0`) and both are monic of degree `r`, `m_β = f#`. In particular `f#` is irreducible.

(v) Put `D = p^{d−k}`. Since `λ = μ^P` and the exponents commute (`P·(1 + D) = (1 + D)·P` as integers), `(μ^{1+D})^P = μ^{P(1+D)} = (μ^P)^{1+D} = λ^{1+D}`. Therefore `λ^{1+D} = 1 ⟺ (μ^{1+D})^P = 1`. In a field of characteristic `p`, `y^P = 1` implies `y = 1`, because `y^P − 1 = (y − 1)^P` (the binomial coefficients `C(P, i)`, `0 < i < P`, are divisible by `p` as `P` is a power of `p`) and a field has no nilpotents. Hence `(μ^{1+D})^P = 1 ⟺ μ^{1+D} = 1`, proving the equivalence.

(vi) Let `f ∈ F` and `α` a root of `f` in `\bar K`; then `α^{n_0} = μ` (as `f | S`). By (iv), `τ(f) = f#` is the minimal polynomial of `β = Φ(α)^{-1}` over `K`. Moreover `β^{n_0} = Φ(α)^{−n_0} = Φ(α^{n_0})^{-1} = ρ(μ)^{-1}`. By (S3) and (v), `μ^{1 + p^{d−k}} = 1`, i.e. `ρ(μ) = μ^{p^{d−k}} = μ^{-1}`, so `β^{n_0} = μ`. Thus `β` is a root of the separable polynomial `S`, so its minimal polynomial `τ(f)` divides `S` (a minimal polynomial of a root of `S` divides `S`, and separability is what makes the factors of `S` distinct); since `τ(f)` is monic irreducible (iv), `τ(f) ∈ F`. So `τ : F → F`. For bijectivity, define `τ′` exactly as `τ` but with `σ` in place of `ρ` (i.e. `(S6)` with `σ(c_0)^{-1} Σ σ(c_i) x^{r−i}`). The argument of (iv) with `Ψ(t) = t^{p^k}` (extending `σ`) shows `τ′(g)` is the minimal polynomial of `γ^{−p^k}` for any root `γ` of `g`. Taking `g = τ(f)` and `γ = β`, we get that `τ′τ(f)` is the minimal polynomial of `β^{−p^k} = Φ(α)^{p^k} = α^{p^{d−k}·p^k} = α^{p^d}`. Since `K = F_{p^d}`, the map `t ↦ t^{p^d}` fixes `K` pointwise, maps `α` to another root of `f`, and preserves degrees exactly as in (iv); hence the minimal polynomial of `α^{p^d}` over `K` is `f` itself. Therefore `τ′τ(f) = f` for all `f ∈ F`; the symmetric computation gives `ττ′(g) = g`. So `τ` is a permutation of the finite set `F` with inverse `τ′`. Finally, `deg τ(f) = deg f` by (ii), so all factors in a `τ`-orbit have equal degree. ∎

**Theorem T2 (reciprocal, single component).** Under (S1)–(S6): (i) `#` is multiplicative on monic polynomials with nonzero constant term, and in particular `(∏_{f∈F} f^{a_f})# = ∏_{f∈F} (f#)^{a_f}`; (ii) if `f(α) = 0` for `α ∈ \bar K`, `α ≠ 0`, then `f#(α^{−p^{d−k}}) = 0`; (iii) `λ^{1+p^{d−k}} = 1 ⟺ μ^{1+p^{d−k}} = 1`, and under (S3) the map `τ = #` permutes `F` preserving degrees.

**Proof.** (i) is Lemma RR-2(iii); (ii) is RR-2(iv) (with `α ≠ 0` guaranteed when `f(0) ≠ 0`); (iii) is RR-2(v)–(vi). ∎

## 3. RR-3 — The Euclidean-dual generator lemma (no separability)

This lemma uses none of the repeated-root structure. We isolate exactly what it needs.

**Lemma RR-3.** Let `n ≥ 1`, `λ ∈ K*`, and let `g ∈ K[x]` be a monic divisor of `x^n − λ` (no condition on `n`, on `g`, or on separability). Put `r = deg g`, `h = (x^n − λ)/g`, `s = deg h = n − r`, and let `C = ⟨g⟩ = {gu : deg(gu) < n} ⊆ K^n` under the coefficient identification. Define the normalized reciprocal  
`h^♮(x) = h_0^{-1} Σ_{i=0}^s h_i x^{s−i}` where `h = Σ h_i x^i` (note `h_0 ≠ 0`, proved below).  
Then the Euclidean dual satisfies `C^⊥ = ⟨h^♮⟩ = {h^♮ v : deg(h^♮ v) < n}`, and `h^♮` is a monic divisor of `x^n − λ^{-1}`; i.e. `C^⊥` is the `λ^{-1}`-constacyclic code with generator `h^♮`.

**Proof.** Since `gh = x^n − λ`, comparing constant terms gives `g_0 h_0 = −λ ≠ 0`; hence `h_0 ≠ 0` (and `g_0 ≠ 0`), so `h^♮` is well defined, monic of degree `s`.

*Dimension preliminaries.* The shifts `g, xg, …, x^{n−r−1}g` have distinct degrees `r, r+1, …, n−1`, hence are linearly independent, and every multiple of `g` of degree `< n` is a `K`-combination of them; so `dim_K C = n − r`. Identically, the multiples of `h^♮` of degree `< n` form a space of dimension `n − s = r`. Also, `dim_K C^⊥ = n − dim_K C = r`: indeed, if `B` is a basis of `C` written as rows of a `(dim C) × n` matrix, then `C^⊥` is the kernel of the linear map `K^n → K^{dim C}`, `z ↦ (Σ_i c_i z_i)_{c ∈ B}`; that map is surjective (its rows are linearly independent) and its kernel is exactly `C^⊥` (orthogonality to a basis is orthogonality to `C`), so rank–nullity gives `dim C^⊥ = n − dim C`.

*The candidate divides `x^n − λ^{-1}`.* For a polynomial `φ` of degree `t`, write `φ^*(x) = x^t φ(1/x)` (unnormalized reciprocal). If `deg φ_1 = t_1`, `deg φ_2 = t_2`, then `(φ_1 φ_2)^* = φ_1^* φ_2^*` (both sides equal `x^{t_1 + t_2} φ_1(1/x) φ_2(1/x)`). Applying this to `gh = x^n − λ`: `g^* h^* = (x^n − λ)^* = x^n((1/x)^n − λ) = 1 − λx^n = −λ(x^n − λ^{-1})`. Hence `h^* | x^n − λ^{-1}` (the factor `−λ ∈ K*` is a unit), and `h^♮ = h_0^{-1} h^*` is a unit multiple of `h^*`, so `h^♮ | x^n − λ^{-1}` as well.

*Orthogonality (inclusion `⟨h^♮⟩ ⊆ C^⊥`).* Two trivial cases first. If `r = 0`, then `g = 1`, `C = K^n`, `C^⊥ = {0}`, and `h^♮` has degree `s = n`, whose only multiple of degree `< n` is `0`; equality holds. If `s = 0`, then `h = 1`, `g = x^n − λ`, `C = {0}`, `C^⊥ = K^n`, and `h^♮ = 1`, whose multiples of degree `< n` are all of `K^n`; equality holds. Assume now `r, s ≥ 1`.

For `a, d ∈ K[x]` of degree `< n`, define the reversal `rev_n(d)(x) = x^{n−1} d(1/x) = Σ_{i=0}^{n−1} d_i x^{n−1−i}`. Then the Euclidean pairing is a single coefficient:
```
⟨a, d⟩_E := Σ_{i=0}^{n−1} a_i d_i = [x^{n−1}] ( a(x)·rev_n(d)(x) ),        (1)
```
because the coefficient of `x^{n−1}` in `a(x)·rev_n(d)(x)` is `Σ_i a_i · [x^{n−1−i}] rev_n(d) = Σ_i a_i d_i`.

Take `a ∈ C` and `d ∈ ⟨h^♮⟩`; write `a = gu` with `deg u < s` (possible since `deg a < n = r + s` and `g | a`) and `d = h^♮ v` with `deg v < r`. Put `V(x) = x^{r−1} v(1/x)` (a polynomial of degree `≤ r − 1`, since `deg v ≤ r − 1`; if `v = 0` then `d = 0` and there is nothing to prove). We compute `rev_n(d)`. First,
`h^♮(1/x) = h_0^{-1} Σ_i h_i (1/x)^{s−i} = h_0^{-1} x^{−s} Σ_i h_i x^i = h_0^{-1} x^{−s} h(x)`.  
Therefore
```
rev_n(d)(x) = x^{n−1} h^♮(1/x) v(1/x)
            = h_0^{-1} x^{n−1−s} h(x) v(1/x)
            = h_0^{-1} x^{r−1} h(x) · x^{−(r−1)} V(x)        (since n − 1 − s = r − 1)
            = h_0^{-1} h(x) V(x).                            (2)
```
Hence, using `a = gu` and `gh = x^n − λ`,
```
a(x)·rev_n(d)(x) = h_0^{-1} (x^n − λ)·(u(x) V(x)).           (3)
```
Now `deg u ≤ s − 1` and `deg V ≤ r − 1`, so `w := uV` satisfies `deg w ≤ n − 2` (or `w = 0`). In `(x^n − λ)w`, the term `x^n w` has only degrees `≥ n` (or vanishes), and the term `−λw` has only degrees `≤ n − 2`; therefore the coefficient of `x^{n−1}` is `0`. By (1), `⟨a, d⟩_E = 0`. Since `a ∈ C` and `d ∈ ⟨h^♮⟩` were arbitrary, `⟨h^♮⟩ ⊆ C^⊥`.

*Equality.* Both spaces have dimension `r` (dimension preliminaries), and one is contained in the other; finite-dimensional spaces with an inclusion and equal dimension are equal. Hence `C^⊥ = ⟨h^♮⟩`. ∎

**Conditions used by RR-3 (complete list):** `λ ∈ K*`; `g` monic with `g | x^n − λ`; the coefficient identification of codes with polynomials of degree `< n`. Nothing else: in particular **not** `gcd(n, p) = 1`, **not** `n = Pn_0`, **not** compatibility, **not** separability of `x^n − λ`. The proof uses only the identity `gh = x^n − λ`, the reversal identity (1)–(2), the degree bounds `deg u < s`, `deg v < r`, and dimension counts.

## 4. Transport by `ρ`, and RR-4 (command): the Galois dual generator

**Lemma 4.1 (transport of codes by a coefficient automorphism).** Let `φ ∈ {σ, ρ}` and let `φ̄` be the coefficientwise map of Lemma RR-2(i) (for `φ = ρ`) resp. its analogue for `σ`. If `E = ⟨e⟩ ⊆ K[x]/(x^n − η)` with `e` a monic divisor of `x^n − η`, then `φ̄(E) = ⟨φ̄(e)⟩ ⊆ K[x]/(x^n − φ(η))`.

**Proof.** `φ̄` is a ring automorphism of `K[x]` (Lemma RR-2(i) for `ρ`; the same coefficient computation for `σ`), and `φ̄(x^n − η) = x^n − φ(η)` because `φ̄` fixes `1` and the indeterminate’s coefficients. Hence `φ̄` maps the ideal `(e)` onto `(φ̄(e))` and the ideal `(x^n − η)` onto `(x^n − φ(η))`, inducing the claimed identification of quotient ideals. Elementwise: `d = ev` with `deg d < n` implies `φ̄(d) = φ̄(e)φ̄(v)` with `deg φ̄(v) = deg v` (a field automorphism sends the nonzero leading coefficient of `v` to a nonzero element), so multiples of degree `< n` correspond exactly. ∎

**Proposition 4.2 (blueprint RR-4: twist transport).** For any `K`-linear code `C ⊆ K^n`, with `D(C)` and `C^⊥` as in (S7),
```
D(C) = ρ(C^⊥)   (coordinatewise ρ applied to the Euclidean dual).
```
Consequently, if `C = ⟨g⟩ ⊆ K[x]/(x^n − λ)` and `h = (x^n − λ)/g`, then `D(C) = ⟨h#⟩ ⊆ K[x]/(x^n − λ^{−p^{d−k}})`. If moreover `λ` is compatible (S3), then `λ^{−p^{d−k}} = λ`, so `D(C) = ⟨h#⟩ ⊆ K[x]/(x^n − λ)`.

**Proof.** By (S7), `y ∈ D(C)` iff for every `c ∈ C`, `Σ_{i=0}^{n−1} c_i σ(y_i) = 0`. Put `w = σ(y)` (coordinatewise `σ`); this says exactly `Σ_i c_i w_i = 0` for all `c ∈ C`, i.e. `w ∈ C^⊥`. Since `σ` is a bijection of `K^n` with inverse `ρ`, `y ∈ D(C) ⟺ σ(y) ∈ C^⊥ ⟺ y ∈ ρ(C^⊥)`. This proves the displayed identity — this is the exact point where the argument order in (S7) is used: the candidate sits in the `σ`-slot, so solving for the candidate applies `σ^{-1} = ρ` to the Euclidean dual.

Now apply Lemma RR-3: `C^⊥ = ⟨h^♮⟩ ⊆ K[x]/(x^n − λ^{-1})`. By Lemma 4.1 with `φ = ρ`, `D(C) = ρ(C^⊥) = ⟨ρ̄(h^♮)⟩ ⊆ K[x]/(x^n − ρ(λ^{-1}))`. We identify the generator: `ρ(λ^{-1}) = ρ(λ)^{-1} = (λ^{p^{d−k}})^{-1} = λ^{−p^{d−k}}`, and
`ρ̄(h^♮) = ρ(h_0)^{-1} Σ_{i=0}^s ρ(h_i) x^{s−i} = h#`  
by comparing with (S6) applied to `h` (which is legitimate: `h` is monic and `h_0 ≠ 0` by RR-3). Finally, compatibility (S3) says `λ^{1 + p^{d−k}} = 1`, i.e. `λ^{p^{d−k}} = λ^{-1}`, hence `λ^{−p^{d−k}} = λ`. ∎

**Theorem T3 (code-first dual, single component).** Under (S1)–(S6), for every assignment `a`, with `h_a = M/g_a`,
```
D(C(a)) = ⟨h_a#⟩ ⊆ K[x]/(x^n − λ^{−p^{d−k}}),
```
and under compatibility (S3) `D(C(a)) = ⟨h_a#⟩ ⊆ K[x]/(x^n − λ)`. The exponents of `h_a#` are: for each `τ`-orbit `O = (f_0, …, f_{L−1})` (S8) and `α_i = a_{f_i}`, the exponent of `f_i` in `h_a#` is
```
β_i = P − α_{i−1}        (indices mod L).
```

**Proof.** Apply Proposition 4.2 to `C = C(a)`, `g = g_a`, `h = h_a` (RR-3 applies by (S5): `g_a` is a monic divisor of `x^n − λ` and `h_a = (x^n − λ)/g_a`). This gives the generator statement and the twist statement. For the exponents: by (S5), `h_a = ∏_{f∈F} f^{P − a_f}`. By Theorem T2(i) and Lemma RR-2(vi) (`τ` permutes `F`),
`h_a# = ∏_{f∈F} (τ(f))^{P − a_f} = ∏_{g∈F} g^{P − a_{τ^{-1}(g)}}` (reindexing `g = τ(f)`).  
Fix an orbit `O` and `g = f_i`. Then `τ^{-1}(f_i) = f_{i−1}` by (S8), so the exponent of `f_i` in `h_a#` is `P − a_{f_{i−1}} = P − α_{i−1} =: β_i`. ∎

*Remark (what compatibility is and is not needed for).* The identity `D(C(a)) = ⟨h_a#⟩` and the exponent formula `β_i = P − α_{i−1}` hold **without** compatibility; compatibility is used only in the last sentence of Proposition 4.2 to identify the ambient modulus `x^n − λ^{−p^{d−k}}` with `x^n − λ`, i.e. to regard `D(C(a))` as a `λ`-constacyclic code in the same quotient. This distinction matters in §6.

## 5. RR-5 — The lcm lemma in a PID and in the quotient

**Lemma RR-5.** (i) For nonzero `g, l ∈ K[x]`, `(g) ∩ (l) = (lcm(g, l))`, where `lcm` is the monic least common multiple.  
(ii) Let `g, l` be monic divisors of a monic `M ∈ K[x]`, and let `π : K[x] → K[x]/(M)` be the quotient map. Then `π((g)) ∩ π((l)) = π((lcm(g, l)))` (and `lcm(g, l) | M`).  
(iii) If `g = ∏_π π^{u_π}` and `l = ∏_π π^{v_π}` are the factorizations of Fact 0.2, then `lcm(g, l) = ∏_π π^{max(u_π, v_π)}`.

**Proof.** (iii) By Fact 0.2, a polynomial `m` is a common multiple of `g` and `l` iff `v_π(m) ≥ u_π` and `v_π(m) ≥ v_π` for every irreducible `π`, iff `v_π(m) ≥ max(u_π, v_π)` for all `π`. The monic polynomial with `v_π = max(u_π, v_π)` is therefore a common multiple dividing every common multiple; it is the `lcm`, which proves (iii) and also the divisibility characterization used next.

(i) `x ∈ (g) ∩ (l)` iff `g | x` and `l | x`, i.e. iff `x` is a common multiple of `g` and `l`. By the previous paragraph, the common multiples are exactly the multiples of `lcm(g, l)`. Hence `(g) ∩ (l) = (lcm(g, l))`.

(ii) Put `m = lcm(g, l)`. Since `g | M` and `l | M`, `M` is a common multiple of `g` and `l`, so `m | M` by the characterization in (iii). In particular `(M) ⊆ (g)`, `(M) ⊆ (l)`, and `(M) ⊆ (m)`. We show the two sides of the claimed equality have the same elements. If `y ∈ π((m))`, write `y = π(Y)` with `Y ∈ (m)`; since `(m) ⊆ (g)` and `(m) ⊆ (l)` (as `g | m` and `l | m`), `y ∈ π((g)) ∩ π((l))`. Conversely, let `y ∈ π((g)) ∩ π((l))` and choose any representative `Y ∈ K[x]` with `π(Y) = y`. Membership `y ∈ π((g))` means `Y = G + Z` with `G ∈ (g)` and `Z ∈ (M)`; but `(M) ⊆ (g)`, so `Y ∈ (g)`. Identically, `Y ∈ (l)`. Hence `Y ∈ (g) ∩ (l) = (m)` by (i), so `y = π(Y) ∈ π((m))`. ∎

*Applicability (precise).* Lemma RR-5(ii) needs exactly: `K[x]` a PID/UFD (Facts 0.1–0.2) and `g, l` both dividing the **same** modulus `M`. In the application below, `g = g_a` and `l = h_a#` both divide `M = x^n − λ`: for `g_a` by construction (S5), and for `h_a#` by Theorem T3 under compatibility. Without compatibility, `h_a#` divides `x^n − λ^{−p^{d−k}}` instead, and (ii) cannot be applied in the single quotient `K[x]/(M)` — see §6.

## 6. RR-4 (command) / Theorem T4 — Hull generator and dimension

**Theorem T4 (hull, single component).** Under (S1)–(S6) including compatibility (S3), for every assignment `a`:
```
Hull_k(C(a)) = ⟨ lcm(g_a, h_a#) ⟩ ⊆ K[x]/(x^n − λ),
```
the exponent of `f_i` in the hull generator is `max(α_i, P − α_{i−1})` (orbit notation of (S8)), and
```
dim_K C(a)      = Σ_{O} deg(f_O) · Σ_{i=0}^{L_O−1} (P − α_i),
dim_K Hull_k(C(a)) = Σ_{O} deg(f_O) · Σ_{i=0}^{L_O−1} min(P − α_i, α_{i−1}),
```
where the outer sums are over all `τ`-orbits `O` of `F`, `f_O` is any factor in `O` (all have the same degree, Lemma RR-2(vi)), and `L_O = |O|`. The blueprint’s `dim_{F_q}` formulas are these with `deg(f_O)` replaced by `w_O = m_s·deg_{K_s}(f_O)` and summed over components `s`.

**Proof.** By definition (S7), `Hull_k(C(a)) = C(a) ∩ D(C(a))` as subspaces of `K^n`, i.e. as ideals of `R = K[x]/(M)`. By Theorem T1, `C(a) = π((g_a))`; by Theorem T3 (compatible case), `D(C(a)) = π((h_a#))`, with both `g_a | M` and `h_a# | M`. Lemma RR-5(ii) therefore gives  
`Hull_k(C(a)) = π((g_a)) ∩ π((h_a#)) = π((lcm(g_a, h_a#))) = ⟨lcm(g_a, h_a#)⟩`.  
By Lemma RR-5(iii) and the exponent computations of (S5) and Theorem T3, the exponent of an irreducible `f` in `lcm(g_a, h_a#)` is `max(v_f(g_a), v_f(h_a#))`; on an orbit `O`, `v_{f_i}(g_a) = α_i` and `v_{f_i}(h_a#) = β_i = P − α_{i−1}`, giving the exponent `max(α_i, P − α_{i−1})`.

For the dimensions, Lemma RR-1(iii) applied to the monic divisors `g_a` and `lcm(g_a, h_a#)` of `M` gives `dim_K C(a) = n − deg g_a` and `dim_K Hull_k(C(a)) = n − deg lcm(g_a, h_a#)`. Now `n = deg M = Σ_{f∈F} P·deg f` (S4), `deg g_a = Σ_f a_f·deg f`, and `deg lcm(g_a, h_a#) = Σ_f max(a_f, β_f)·deg f`. Grouping the factors by `τ`-orbits (constant degree on each orbit, Lemma RR-2(vi)):
```
dim_K C(a) = Σ_{f∈F} (P − a_f)·deg f = Σ_O deg(f_O) Σ_{i=0}^{L_O−1} (P − α_i),
dim_K Hull_k(C(a)) = Σ_{f∈F} [P − max(a_f, β_f)]·deg f.
```
For each orbit index `i`, with `u = α_i` and `v = P − α_{i−1}`, the elementary identity `P − max(u, v) = min(P − u, P − v)` (both sides equal `P − u` if `u ≥ v` and `P − v` otherwise) and `P − v = α_{i−1}` give `P − max(α_i, P − α_{i−1}) = min(P − α_i, α_{i−1})`. Substituting yields the displayed hull formula. The `F_q` statement follows from `[K : F_q] = m_s` applied componentwise (S8/A10 bookkeeping). ∎

**Corollary (code and hull dimensions, factorwise form).** With `β_f` denoting the exponent of `f` in `h_a#` (Theorem T3),  
`dim_K C(a) = Σ_{f∈F} (P − a_f)·deg f` and `dim_K Hull_k(C(a)) = Σ_{f∈F} [P − max(a_f, β_f)]·deg f`.  
This is the computation actually used in the cross-checks of §9.
